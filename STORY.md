# Starseed story summary and introduction

Draft treatment, 8 October 2026. The central black hole and the enclosed star system are separate objects. The sphere is a perfect external mirror that blocks entry. The daughter investigates anomalous readings and chooses to cross an opening. A transmission recorded at the same time launches the search.

This is a hopeful game. The daughter will be fine; the rescue leads toward reunion, discovery and understanding. The crew can be frightened by losing contact without the story threatening to punish the player with her death. Exploration does not run down a hidden survival timer.

The emotional centre is a home you bring along. The fleet offers familiar rooms, useful routines and people who look after one another. Outside is space worth stopping to look at. “Cozy exploration” describes that comfort and pace, while the scale of the setting and optional difficulty of piloting leave room for awe and challenge. Give the player reasons to feel hopeful early, and let curiosity support the search rather than compete with a manufactured emergency.

Names, ages, exact family relationships, dialogue and the length of the introduction remain adjustable. The shared survey craft carrying other expedition family members is the proposed multiplayer version; their safe recovery follows the same hopeful direction. The interior of the sphere and the means of reunion remain to be written.

## Underlying history (author knowledge, not the opening Story Log)

The first intelligent species in this galaxy found itself alone. Its people travelled to worlds
where life existed, or had existed, hoping to meet another mind. Their observatories, biological
archives, experiments and long-lived outposts account for the megastructures and smaller remnants
in these systems. Some destinations are now barren; a vanished biosphere is as relevant to the
investigation as a living forest. Mere habitability is not proof that a world hosted life.

Across immense spans of time they studied evolution, seeded life and experimented with genetic
engineering. None of it brought them another intelligent species with which to share the galaxy.
Eventually they built a temporal refuge around their home system: the silver mirror sphere.
Between brief maintenance and communication intervals, no subjective time passes inside. They
hoped to wait for intelligence to evolve naturally elsewhere.

Roughly every thousand external years, the shield opens small apertures to exchange messages
with outposts. The daughter is extraordinarily lucky to be investigating one of those places as
an opening appears. She chooses to enter; she did not trigger it and was not selected by it.
The outgoing transmission and her crossing coincide because both use that communication interval.
Her time inside need not match the expedition's time outside. She will be fine.

Some of the ancient machinery has failed. Other civilizations have risen and disappeared while
the founders waited, unable to enter the sphere or awaken its inhabitants. Evidence of those
civilizations is a later story branch, not a required early exposition dump.

Whether the founders lacked FTL or developed only slow, limited FTL remains undecided. Both
support a geographically limited outpost network and communications constrained by travel time.
Do not imply they had instantaneous galaxy-wide contact or silently choose one explanation yet.

The central black hole, the silver temporal enclosure, and the smaller black spheres above
individual stars are three different classes of object. The smaller spheres' exact role is still
open; the first two sites reveal that they exist, not the whole solution to the mystery.

## Opening investigation and map reveals

1. Follow the recorded transmission to the receiver system.
2. Land on three distinct, interesting planetary bodies and scan a specific phenomenon or trace
   on each. Moons can count; gas giants cannot satisfy a landing requirement. Choose variety
   (living ecosystem, former biosphere, volcanism, ice, tidal activity, unusual geology), with
   safe, reachable landing/scan locations for every fleet difficulty. Persist the selected targets
   and each scan; revisiting one body cannot count as three observations.
3. Combining all three scans resolves the first megastructure's location. Guided exploration
   can supply clear target markers but does not skip the story's three observations.
4. Exploring the first megastructure reveals approximately three more candidate megastructures,
   preferably offering a choice among nearby systems. The chart shows what the records establish
   and any remaining uncertainty. All three are leads, not a demand to clear every location.
5. Exploring two distinct megastructures in total reveals evidence for a black sphere a number of
   AU above the star in systems that have, or once had, life. The local system map supplies its
   three-dimensional position and a navigation target; the overhead galaxy chart marks the lead
   and its height rather than hiding it in a flat projection. Visit several as the next branch.

For progression, 'explored' means recovering a site's central record, not stepping through its
entrance or accumulating an arbitrary number of optional fragments. The current 200/500-unit
Golden Path hints are separate from these new one-site/two-site gates.

A separate site-variation setting will offer **Authored sites** and **Randomized sites**. The
randomized mode chooses entrance location, theme and layout afresh for each expedition, then
saves those choices. Loading, returning, joining multiplayer or importing a save must not reroll
anything. Flight difficulty, navigation assistance and jetpack assistance remain independent.
The story facts and solvable discovery sequence are shared by both modes.

Implementation status: the map/recipe/cache foundation and former-life eligibility are implemented.
Three-body selection currently exists as a tested ranking helper. Physical scan targets, their
quest gates, the site-variation setting and the smaller black spheres are the next playable work;
they are not yet present in the game.

## Story summary

Your expedition came to this region to study a black hole of extraordinary size. Its luminous disk is visible from nearby star systems. Jets rise out of the galactic centre and continue far beyond the patch of space your instruments have charted. From different worlds, the same structure appears as a narrow blade, a bright knot or a long luminous thread. It becomes a familiar reference during an unfamiliar journey.

The expedition is equipped for years away from home. Its ships are laboratories, workshops and living quarters. Families are part of the community. In multiplayer, the player characters belong to that same expedition and share its discoveries, fleet and search for the missing tender.

While examining a system whose relationship to the central object deserves closer study, the team discovers a sphere surrounding it. There is no visible star. There are no planetary transits to watch. Across the sphere, the black hole, jets and surrounding stars are reflected with a precision the instruments cannot distinguish from perfection. Light and radio return from the outside. Probes cannot pass through it.

Gravity suggests substantial mass within. The expedition suspects an enclosed star system, but cannot directly observe its interior. Perhaps the sphere shields it from the central object's radiation. Perhaps the relationship is more complicated. Several years of research produce accurate measurements, familiar survey routes and no way in.

Your daughter is part of that work. On a routine survey flight, she finds a small region giving readings the team has never seen before. She approaches in an expedition tender. Other family members may be aboard, giving each player a personal connection to the people who disappear.

An aperture forms. For the first time, the instruments receive information from beyond the surface. She believes the opening may close before the team can investigate it. You tell her to wait. She crosses.

The mirror seals behind the craft. Its telemetry and communications stop. Repeated calls, scans and attempts to find the opening produce nothing.

The expedition's monitoring network recorded a brief electromagnetic transmission during the event. Reconstructing its direction identifies another star system as a likely destination. The team suspects that the opening served a communication function and that the tender happened to enter during that interval. If the receiver is part of the same system, it might contain a way to contact the sphere or make it open again.

You leave to follow the transmission. The investigation leads to immense structures beneath worlds with present or past life: damaged machinery, abandoned service spaces, surviving power and fragments of a distributed record. The recovered data begins to describe a network called the Golden Path. Learning enough to understand its access rules offers a possible route back to the missing people.

The rescue gives the expedition a purpose, while the galaxy remains open. Players may pursue side discoveries, return to familiar worlds or take different routes through the same investigation. The black hole stays visible from much of the initial region, and the mirrored system remains a place they can revisit.

Current implementation: New Expedition offers real flight practice followed by interactive survey telemetry for the incident and signal analysis, or a convergent skip. This is a playable rehearsal of the teaching/story sequence. The physical mirror surface, aperture crossing, daughter’s craft choreography and central black-hole scene described below are still planned.

## Playable introduction treatment

The cockpit glass darkens as the research ship turns.

Beyond its hull, the accretion disk curls around the black centre. One jet is brighter than the other from this angle. The ship's instruments pick out structure farther along it than your eyes can follow. A little to the side, another image of the disk bends across the mirror sphere.

The two are familiar enough that you check the flight instruments first.

Your daughter calls from the tender ahead.

“You coming?”

Her craft is a small moving light against the reflection. It carries survey equipment and, in the multiplayer version, other members of the expedition's families. This is a regular run along a surface the team has measured for years.

You clear the research ship, turn toward her transponder and match velocity. She waits while you settle into formation. A brief exchange about the next survey track makes it clear that you have flown together before. There is time to get comfortable with the controls, use the navigation computer or ask for another explanation.

The survey computer requests the first reading. You hold position while it integrates. The sphere returns the expected result: external reflection, no signal from within.

Then her transponder moves away from the next planned station.

“I'm getting something here.”

You select her scan. A small patch has stopped matching the surrounding return. At first it looks like an instrument disagreement. She adjusts her angle. The anomaly stays on the surface.

“Hold there. Let's get a second baseline.”

She slows. For a moment you are both watching a reflection so smooth that distance is hard to judge. Your display supplies the range that the view cannot.

A section of the reflected jet breaks.

The patch opens without a visible door or moving panel. Across it, the external reflection is gone. Her instruments report a space ahead where a solid boundary should be. The aperture's size and the next few readings are enough to suggest that the tender could pass through; they are not enough to establish that it is safe.

“There's room. I can take a look.”

“No. Stay outside.”

“If it closes—”

“We'll mark it. Wait for me.”

Her engines brighten. You accelerate toward her, still with control of your own craft. The separation and her head start make the situation clear before the opening begins to change. Your instruments are already recording; the expedition is calling both ships.

Her transponder crosses the surface.

“I'm through. There's—”

The transmission ends. The reflection joins across the patch, leaving the jet unbroken again.

You arrive at the coordinates. There is no opening to follow. Range returns are ordinary. Her transponder does not answer. You can move along the surface, repeat the scan or keep calling. The crew gives you time to try.

Eventually an analyst calls from the research ship.

“We caught another transmission. It wasn't hers.”

The probes around the sphere recorded different parts of the event. The player brings their observations together in the scanner display. After correction for the probes' positions and light travel time, the results define an outgoing direction.

The galactic map opens overhead. The sphere, research ship and central black hole establish the view. A bearing extends toward a charted system. Its uncertainty narrows as the observations are combined.

“We think something there was meant to receive it.”

You ask whether the opening can be made to happen again.

“We don't know. But this is the first thing it's done that points somewhere we can go.”

The Story Log records the disappearance, the evidence and the crew's hypothesis. A new lead appears: Investigate the transmission destination.

You select the system and plot a route. The fleet has work to do before departure: establish the departure fix, bring the tender's last telemetry aboard and prepare the ships for travel. These tasks introduce the fleet interface. The crew concentrates on what it can learn and do next; there is no hidden rescue timer.

The introduction ends when the expedition is ready to leave. Players may depart, review the evidence, practise a control or return to the sphere for another look.

## Introductory beats and controls

| Beat | Player action | Story or teaching purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Familiar survey | Undock, orient and match the tender's velocity | Establish the expedition and relationship; teach translation, braking, target selection and optional guidance. |
| Baseline reading | Hold a survey position and operate the scanner | Show normal behaviour before the anomaly. |
| Anomalous patch | Inspect the daughter's reading and approach | Teach range and relative motion; establish her reason for investigating. |
| Crossing | Follow, call or keep scanning | Preserve control while the authored incident occurs. No quick-time prompt or misleading Save Her objective. |
| Closed surface | Inspect the last coordinates and attempt contact | Allow a short, responsive search; avoid an endless interaction that silently blocks progress. |
| Transmission analysis | Combine already-recorded observations | Give the first useful action after the disappearance and explain the evidence. |
| Departure preparation | Review the Story Log, select a destination and plot a route | Introduce the overhead galaxy map, fleet and shared investigation. |

Use ship positions, velocities and the established separation to stage the incident. Do not simply switch off the player's engines or force a collision when they try to follow. Rehearse the geometry for all starter fleets. Treat alternate player positions and early arrivals as explicit scene cases; make the aperture close before a following player reaches its crossing plane. If a rare state cannot be resolved fairly, restore the last tutorial checkpoint with a clear explanation rather than hiding an arbitrary physical wall.

Ordinary flight mistakes before the incident get a safe retry and optional assistance. The family's disappearance is an authored event, not a judgement of the player's flying. Do not add a rescue countdown or make side exploration endanger the daughter. Later evidence should sustain hope through real discoveries, without repeated false death scares.

The playable introduction is single-player, including flight practice and the incident. Multiplayer becomes available immediately after completing or skipping it. A friend then joins the expedition in an assigned craft, or accompanies the other player where supported, and receives the shared Story Log without replaying the incident. The other families can still have people aboard the survey tender; their motivation does not require a co-op tutorial. Essential dialogue has subtitles and remains in the record.

## Skipped introduction

Skipping completes the canonical incident transition once, places the fleet at a safe departure staging point, activates the first lead and writes the entries below. It does not award extra resources or remove assets beyond the same authored missing tender. Tutorial actions remain available in Basics. A full replay later runs as a separate practice scenario so it cannot duplicate campaign rewards or rewrite the family's status.

### Expedition record

**Why we came**

Our expedition came to study the enormous black hole and its outflows. During that work we discovered a nearby system enclosed by a reflective barrier. We have spent several years measuring it. Incoming probes and electromagnetic signals cannot penetrate it. Its purpose and interior remain unknown.

**The missing tender**

A survey flight found anomalous readings on the mirror. An opening formed, and the tender entered while investigating. The opening closed and contact stopped. Your daughter was aboard. In the shared expedition, other missing passengers include family members of the crew.

**The transmission**

The survey network recorded a separate electromagnetic burst during the opening. Its direction points toward a nearby system. We suspect the opening was associated with that communication, but have not established how it worked or whether the receiver can open it again.

### Current lead

**Investigate the transmission destination**

Travel to the system identified by the survey network. Look for evidence of the transmission's receiver and any connection to the mirrored enclosure. The destination is marked on the galactic map.

### Basics available immediately

Flight controls and assists; relative velocity and braking; navigation computer orders; docking and carried craft; conventional travel, FTL and shared time warp; galactic charts and route confidence; scanning; megastructure discovery and traversal; Story Log and save/export controls.

## Clues and unresolved questions

The signal's source, direction, timing and recorded spectrum are evidence. Its recipient, meaning and relationship to the opening are hypotheses until investigated. Correct observations for light travel time; FTL travel may overtake the pulse. Older records at the destination can match its signature before the current pulse arrives.

The first Golden Path records establish shared addressing and a deeper network. They need not immediately explain the mirror or identify its builders. The daughter's safety is an authorial commitment even before the crew obtains reassuring evidence; deciding when that evidence arrives is a pacing choice. Do not decide that the black hole powers the enclosure merely because the image suggests it. Preserve room for discovery in how these things relate.

Later writing must establish the crew and missing passengers, why the daughter believes entry is worth the risk, what evidence sustains the rescue effort, what the network actually does and how access can be requested or recovered. The player's first objective is concrete even while those answers remain open.
