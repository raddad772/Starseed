# Vertical megastructures: ideas for Dave to pick (2026-10-14)

Dave (2026-10-09): "going down a crack between buildings", "suuuuper deep": you start out deep and go REALLY
deep, deep enough that gravity changes on the way down (a shell's or a hollow world's own gravity: less the
deeper you go?). Ideas first.

## What really happens to gravity on the way down

At game scale every world keeps its surface gravity but is drawn at 1/64 of its size (Earth 100 km across
the radius, Mars 52, the Moon 27, Ganymede 42, Enceladus 4). So a shaft a few kilometres deep is a real
fraction of a small world, and the change is physical, not a gimmick:

- **A world of even density** (the Moon, most rocky moons): gravity falls in step with the distance to the
  centre. Half-way down, half the weight; at the centre, none.
- **A world with a dense core** (Earth, Mars): gravity first *rises* a little going down (Earth's is ~10%
  stronger at its core's edge), then falls to nothing at the centre.
- **An icy moon** (Europa, Ganymede, Enceladus): its ice is lighter than its rock below, so gravity *rises*
  noticeably going down through the ice shell (Ganymede: ~1.4 to ~1.8 m/s2 at the rock), then falls.
- **A hollow world or shell** (built, not natural): gravity falls through the shell's thickness and is
  exactly zero anywhere inside (Newton's shell theorem), unless something sits at the centre.
- **Past the centre**, "down" turns round: what was the way down is now up. Walls become floors on the way
  through, the gravity-direction rooms the earlier pass deferred, given a physical reason.

The founders built observatories, archives and experiments on worlds that had or might have life
(STORY.md). Each idea below is one of those, a reason for something that deep to exist.

## The ideas

### 1. The Plumb: a borehole through a small moon (recommended)

A founders' geophysical observatory: a shaft bored straight through a small rocky or icy moon, centre and
all, to measure the world from inside. The entrance is a slot between two of the founders' monolithic
buildings on the surface, the "crack between buildings", going down out of the daylight.

- Down the shaft, stations every few hundred metres: a gauge on each wall reads the local gravity, falling
  (or on an icy moon, first rising) as you go. Jumps get floatier, things you drop fall slower, ladders
  give way to tethers and handholds.
- At the centre: a spherical chamber with no weight at all (the existing zero-g play), the moon's heart
  measured round you. Past it, the far half of the shaft: "down" has turned over, and the climb out the
  other side is a climb up towards the far surface, which was down a moment ago.
- Optional: the far mouth opens on the moon's other side, a long way from where you went in.
- Builds on: the express lift, the Null Well's zero-g, gravity strength by room. New: gravity strength
  from depth (a world's density profile, two layers), gravity direction from the centre (the deferred
  reversal pass, here continuous along the shaft rather than room by room), a long vertical shaft drawn
  through the ground (a borehole volume in the terrain's near field, like the caves).
- Scale: on a 4 km moon (Enceladus-like) the whole crossing is ~8 km, a few minutes by lift and climb.

### 2. The Rift Archive: a crack between buildings, kilometres down

Two kilometre-high walls of the founders' archive stacks with a narrow slot between them, cut down into
the crust. Ledges, cross-bridges and cage lifts between the walls; daylight a thread above. Each tier is
an older era of the archive (the founders' records of a world's life over its history: the strata of
specimens, a vanished biosphere's leaves and bones in the lower tiers).

- Gravity changes little at Earth-like depths (a few km is a few % of the drawn radius), so the change
  here is light, heat and age, not weight. Deep enough on a small world and the gravity starts to show.
- Builds on: the existing themed chambers as rooms off the slot; the generator halls as tier machinery.
  New: a long open vertical space you see down into (the 900 m void, walked rather than seen through
  glass).
- A natural opening for idea 1 (the Rift's floor is the Plumb's mouth), or a site of its own.

### 3. The Hollow Moon: a shell around nothing

A small moon the founders hollowed (or built round a space), its crust a few kilometres thick. Down a
shaft through the shell, gravity fades to zero at the inner surface; inside, a vast weightless hollow lit
by something at its centre (a founders' lamp, a sealed experiment). If the moon spins, the inside's
floor is the inner surface, overhead in every direction: you walk upside down on the world's inside.

- The biggest "wow" of the set; also the biggest build (a second, inverted terrain on the inner surface).
- Builds on: zero-g, the spectral haze. New: an inside-out world (a sphere drawn from inside), spin
  gravity.

### 4. The Ice Well: down through an icy moon's shell to its ocean

A crevasse widened into a well through an ice shell, down to the ocean under it, where a founders'
biological station floats (their archive of that ocean's life). Gravity rises as you go down through the
ice (lighter than the moon's average); blue light through the walls fading to dark; the ocean at the
bottom under pressure.

- Ties to the life work (an ocean's own life, the founders' samples of it) and to the water renderer.
- New: an ocean under a ceiling of ice, swimming or a submersible.

### 5. The Mascon Galleries: chambers beside dense masses

An experiment in gravity itself: down a shaft, chambers built beside enormous dense masses the founders
sank into the rock. Next to one, its pull is sideways: the wall facing it is the floor. Gravity's
direction set by real masses, room by room.

- The most direct route to the deferred walls-become-floors rooms, physically motivated. Smaller than 1.
- New: gravity as a sum of the world's and nearby masses (a few point masses), direction per place.

### 6. The Inverted Tower: an "earthscraper"

A founders' tower built downward: a central atrium void hundreds of metres deep, floors ringed round it,
ramps spiralling down, the deeper floors older. The crack between buildings as its light wells.

- The most like the existing chambers; gravity constant. Good for the time and light themes.

## Recommendation

**The Plumb (1)**, entered by way of **the Rift (2)**. It answers the request most directly ("start out
deep and go REALLY deep, deep enough that gravity changes"), the change is real physics with a clear
readout, it gives the deferred gravity reversal a reason, and it reuses the most of what's built (lift,
zero-g, themed chambers as stations). Hosts: small moons (rocky for "less the deeper you go", icy for the
surprise of more), placed by the same site rules as today's megastructures.

Build order if picked: (a) gravity from depth and direction from a world's centre in the walker's physics
(two-layer density from the body's composition), with a test against the shell theorem and the uniform
body; (b) the shaft as a near-field volume through the ground, stations as chambers; (c) the centre's
zero-g chamber and the turn-over; (d) the Rift's slot and its tiers as the entrance.
